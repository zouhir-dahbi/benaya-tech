**Short answer:** a professional whole-home Wi-Fi installation is generally a few hundred dollars for a small condo or townhome with a straightforward mesh setup, and can run into four figures for a larger house that needs wired access points, structured cabling, or coverage for a detached garage or basement. The real cost driver isn't the equipment — it's how much of the home needs coverage and how much of that requires new wiring.

## What actually drives the price

Two similarly sized homes can have very different Wi-Fi installation costs, because price is driven less by square footage and more by:

- **Number of access points needed.** A single mesh node might cover a small home; a larger or multi-level home may need three or more to eliminate dead zones.
- **Wired vs. wireless backhaul.** Running Ethernet to each access point costs more upfront in labor but is more reliable than relying on wireless mesh alone, especially through brick, concrete, or plaster walls.
- **Existing infrastructure.** A home with existing conduit or Ethernet drops is cheaper to upgrade than one that needs new cable runs through finished walls.
- **Outdoor and detached coverage.** Extending reliable Wi-Fi to a garage, shed, or patio usually adds cost, since it often needs a dedicated access point and sometimes buried cable.
- **Equipment tier.** Consumer mesh kits, business-grade access points, and higher-throughput equipment for 4K streaming or video calls on many devices at once all sit at different price points.

## What a professional installation typically includes

A proper whole-home Wi-Fi project isn't just plugging in a box. It typically covers:

- A walkthrough to identify dead zones, high-usage rooms (home office, media room), and interference sources
- Access point placement based on your home's actual layout and building materials, not guesswork
- Wiring where it improves reliability, run and terminated cleanly
- Network configuration — naming, security settings, guest network, and device prioritization for things like video calls
- A final walkthrough testing coverage in every room, not just near the router

## Signs you need more than a router swap

If any of these sound familiar, the issue is usually coverage or network design, not the router brand:

- Video calls drop in the same one or two rooms every time
- Streaming works fine downstairs but buffers upstairs
- A basement, garage, or addition never gets a usable signal
- Every "no. of bars" Wi-Fi icon looks fine, but speeds still crawl in certain spots

A single new router — even a premium one — doesn't fix a physics problem: a home too large, too far, or too obstructed for one radio to reach everything reliably.

## Getting an accurate number for your home

General ranges are useful for planning, but the only way to get a real number is a walkthrough of your specific home — its size, layout, wall materials, and where you actually need coverage. That's true whether you're calling around for quotes or reading anyone's blog post, including this one.
