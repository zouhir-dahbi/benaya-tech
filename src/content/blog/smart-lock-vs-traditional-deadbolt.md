**Short answer:** a traditional deadbolt is simpler, cheaper, and has no batteries or software to maintain — it just works. A smart lock adds keyless entry, temporary or scheduled access codes, remote lock/unlock, and an entry log, at the cost of batteries to manage and a setup that benefits from being done correctly. The real question isn't "which is more secure" so much as "do you need the access-management features a deadbolt can't offer."

## What doesn't actually change

Security-wise, a smart lock isn't automatically stronger or weaker than a good traditional deadbolt. Many smart locks use the same ANSI-graded lock hardware as their traditional counterparts, or retrofit motorized control onto a standard deadbolt. If physical break-in resistance is your main concern, lock grade matters more than "smart" vs. "traditional."

## What a smart lock actually adds

- **No more physical keys to copy, lose, or re-key.** Access is managed through codes or app permissions instead.
- **Temporary and scheduled access.** Give a cleaner, contractor, or guest a code that only works for a specific window, without handing out a real key.
- **An entry log.** See when the door was unlocked and by which code — useful for peace of mind, not just security incidents.
- **Remote lock/unlock**, useful for letting someone in, or double-checking you locked up, without being home.
- **Integration with other smart-home routines** — locking automatically when an alarm arms, or as part of a "leaving home" scene.

## What's a genuine tradeoff, not a downside to hide

- **Batteries need occasional replacement.** Not a big deal with a reminder system in place, but it's real maintenance a traditional deadbolt doesn't have.
- **Setup quality matters more.** A poorly configured smart lock (weak Wi-Fi at the door, default codes never changed, app notifications ignored) can undercut its own benefits. A traditional deadbolt has no configuration to get wrong.
- **More moving parts.** Motor, batteries, connectivity — more components than a purely mechanical lock, even with backup options built in.

## How to decide

A traditional deadbolt is a fine, complete answer if you don't need to manage access for other people and just want a reliable lock. A smart lock is worth it if any of these are true for your household:

- You regularly give access to a cleaner, dog walker, contractor, or guest and don't want to hand out keys
- You want to know when the door was actually locked, not just assume it
- The door is part of a broader smart-home or security setup you're already building
- You've dealt with lost or unreturned keys before and want to stop re-keying

## Getting it installed right

The features above only hold up if the lock is installed and configured correctly — proper door alignment so the deadbolt isn't straining the motor, a strong-enough Wi-Fi signal at the door for remote features, and codes actually set up and shared securely rather than left on defaults.
